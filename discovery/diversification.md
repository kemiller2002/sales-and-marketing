I think the mistake would be to treat all of our ideas as separate consulting businesses. There are really a handful of underlying capabilities that can be recombined into different offers.

The common thread is:

Find where a complex system is lying to the organization, make the real state visible, constrain what can happen next, and create evidence that the improvement actually worked.

That gives us a much broader consulting surface than “software architecture consulting.”

Tier 1 — Areas I would actively explore

These are the strongest combinations of differentiation, urgency, credibility, and ability to charge meaningful money.

1. AI Engineering Readiness Assessment

Assess whether an organization is actually prepared to use AI to build and maintain software.

Questions:

* Can AI understand the architecture?
* Is domain intent explicit or reconstructed from old code?
* How much undocumented institutional knowledge exists?
* Are tests evidence of requirements or merely surviving behavior?
* Can agents safely modify the system?
* How much context does an agent require to accomplish a task?
* What happens when an AI makes a plausible but incorrect assumption?
* Can the organization determine why an AI made a change?
* Is there an escalation mechanism when evidence is insufficient?

Potential deliverable:

AI Engineering Readiness Score + remediation roadmap

This could become one of our flagship diagnostics.

The larger pattern

I don’t think we should create 44 service pages.

These collapse into six practices:

AI Engineering

Make software environments safe, understandable, and economical for AI agents.

Diagnostic Engineering

Determine why complex software and organizations aren’t behaving as expected.

AI Governance & Evidence

Control what agents can do and prove what happened.

Modernization

Turn legacy systems into explicit, understandable systems capable of being safely evolved.

Investor / Executive Intelligence

Determine whether technology, organizations, and AI strategies actually support the claims being made about them.

AI-Powered Market Exploration

Use structured experimentation to discover what a company should build, sell, or pivot toward.

And underneath all six is essentially the same intellectual property:

state + evidence + constraints + transitions + diagnostics + agents

That is important because it means we’re not diversifying by wandering into unrelated consulting categories.

We’re discovering different markets for the same underlying capability.

What I would test first

I would not start by trying to sell everything.

I would run market-discovery experiments around these first:

1. AI Engineering Readiness Assessment
2. Agent-Ready Repository Assessment
3. Legacy Knowledge Risk Assessment
4. AI Technical Due Diligence
5. AI Technical Debt / Semantic Drift Assessment
6. AI Agent Governance Assessment
7. AI Development Cost Optimization
8. Compliance Evidence Architecture
9. AI Development Operating Model
10. Pivot / Opportunity Discovery Engagement

Those ten let us test several buyers—CTOs, CEOs, PE/investors, compliance leaders, and founders—without requiring ten fundamentally different competencies.

The particularly interesting possibility is that the assessment becomes the entry product.

Assessment
↓
diagnosis
↓
recommended architecture/process change
↓
implementation
↓
training
↓
ongoing monitoring/governance
↓
software/tooling

That creates a much stronger business than selling engineering hours.

And I think there’s another conclusion worth testing: Echelon Foundry may not actually be an “AI consultancy.” It may be better understood as a diagnostic systems company whose current highest-value problem domain happens to be the transition to AI-operated organizations.

That positioning leaves us considerably more room to diversify later without looking like we’ve pivoted into something unrelated.
